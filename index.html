Leading a series

Physics series, ages 13–15

Managing editor at Gdańsk Educational Publishing House. Led content development for a new physics series, in print and digital, with interactive textbooks and teaching materials. Managed 12+ contributors.

The challenge

The publisher’s previous physics series held about 1.5% of the market. Teachers found it too difficult. The goal was a new series for grades 7–8 that students could actually follow.

What I did

  • Digital learning. Led development of the textbook’s interactive edition. Selected PhET simulations (University of Colorado) for each lesson. Redesigned print problems as interactive whiteboard quizzes. Defined requirements for a test generator and tested it with developers and UX designers.
  • Diagnosis. Found the real barrier to adopting the previous series: not that it was too ambitious, as teachers believed, but that its structure was unclear. Redesigned the page layout to fix it.
  • Clarity and engagement. Rewrote explanations in plain language, broke lessons into short steps, worked with designers on clear layouts, and added humorous illustrations and dialogues.
  • Lesson order. Moved topics requiring formula rearrangement later in the course, after students learned the skill in math class. Added step-by-step support.
  • Problem design. Wrote, revised, and sequenced problems, from conceptual questions to multi-step calculations. Added scaffolded practice where students needed an intermediate step.
  • Research. Built a teacher panel, ran 10+ focus groups, and analyzed surveys from 1,150+ teachers.
  • Delivery. Met a fixed deadline despite a mid-project change to the national curriculum, making fast decisions on content and scope.
  • Team. Reorganized the team and built the production process from scratch, bringing in a new co-author and consultants.

What the series includes

Each lesson is self-contained: theory, worked examples, problems, and a simple experiment, all in one place. Lessons open with a question that sparks curiosity and close with “What do you think?” prompts.
