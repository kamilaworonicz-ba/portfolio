Interactive math, one small step at a time.

I’m a math PhD with 10+ years of turning complex ideas into puzzles people want to solve. I design math courses, prototype interactive lessons, and test digital tools with developers. My earlier work is in Polish: puzzle-based courses for ages 6–15, recreational math courses for adults, and a complete physics series, from print and interactive textbooks to teaching materials, used by about 1 in 10 Polish students in grades 7–8. The pieces below build on ideas I first tested with Polish learners, now redesigned in English for online learning.

The challenge

The publisher’s previous physics series held about 1.5% of the market. Teachers found it too difficult. The goal was a new series for grades 7–8 that students could actually follow.

What I did

  • Digital learning. Led development of the textbook’s interactive edition. Selected PhET simulations (University of Colorado) for each lesson. Redesigned print problems as interactive whiteboard quizzes. Defined requirements for a test generator and tested it with developers and UX designers.
  • Clarity and engagement. Rewrote explanations in plain language, broke lessons into short steps, worked with designers on clear layouts, and added humorous illustrations and dialogues.
  • Lesson order. Moved topics requiring formula rearrangement later in the course, after students learned the skill in math class. Added step-by-step support.
  • Problem design. Wrote, revised and sequenced problems from conceptual questions to multi-step calculations, adding scaffolded practice where students needed an intermediate step.
  • Research. Built a teacher panel, ran 10+ focus groups, and analysed surveys from 1,150+ teachers.

What the series includes

Each chapter is a complete lesson: theory, worked examples, problems and a simple experiment. Lessons open with a question that sparks curiosity and close with “What do you think?” prompts.
